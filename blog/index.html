Differential expression analysis is often the first major step in RNA-seq interpretation. It tells us which genes change between two biological conditions, such as treatment versus control, disease versus normal tissue, or responder versus non-responder samples. However, a list of differentially expressed genes is rarely sufficient on its own. Individual genes are noisy, context-dependent, and difficult to interpret without a broader biological framework.

This is where gene set enrichment analysis, or GSEA, becomes useful.

You have run RNA-seq. You have fold changes, p-values, and maybe a volcano plot that looks perfect. Then comes the hard question:

What biology does this actually mean?

This is where functional enrichment comes in. Functional enrichment helps translate gene-level statistics into biological insight. The three names people usually hear are GO, KEGG, and GSEA. They are often treated as competitors, but in reality they do very different jobs.

Understanding how they differ makes enrichment analysis much easier and much more meaningful.
